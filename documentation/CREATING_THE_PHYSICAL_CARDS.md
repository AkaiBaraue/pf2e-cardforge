## Creating the Physical Cards

There are multiple ways to create the physical cards depending on the quality you want. Below are three methods I've used.

### Method 1 - Plain Paper Cards

This is the most basic method and produces simple paper cards. This is a good starting point.

#### Method 1 Tools and Materials
- Access to a printer. For these cards basically any printer will do, there's nothing that require high-quality color printing. I've used office printers in the past and it's been fine
    - NOTE: _Always do a test print with one page before printing a large batch_. If something is off (fx. due to printer settings), it's better to throw out one page of paper than 10
- A way to cut the cards
    - A pair of scissors is the cheapest option
    - A cutting mat, a boxcutter or similar, and a metal ruler. For the metal ruler, I strongly suggest one with anti-slip and a stainless steel cutting edge, otherwise you risk that the ruler moves while you cut. This is what I use, mostly because I had the tools already
    - A paper trimmer. I've not had much luck with the precision of Guillotine-versions, so I would suggest a Rolling Blade version instead. But if you have a Guillotine trimmer that works well, go for it
- (Optional) A corner cutter/rounder. This makes the final result look better, but is by no means required. I highly recommend the "Kadomaru Pro Corner Cutter" (or "Kadomaru Pro NEO Corner Cutter") over other corner cutters. I have tried others and they've dulled after less than 200 laminated cards (see [Method 3 - Laminated Cards](#method-3---laminated-cards)), making it almost impossible to cut cards. The Kadomaru has now chewn through 800 such cards and still cuts like it was new
- (Optional) Card sleeves or card binder pages. Also not required, but helps standardize the feel of the cards and protects the flimsy paper from being crumbled in no time.

#### Method 1 Process

Just as you'd imagine

1. Print the PDFs you've created.
2. Cut the cards along the lines.
3. (Optional) Round the corners.
4. (Optional) Sleeve the cards.



### Method 2 - Construction Paper Enhanced Cards

This method creates more sturdy cards but doesn't work with dual-sided cards.

#### Method 2 Tools and Materials
- Same as [Method 1 Tools and Materials](#method-1-tools-and-materials)
- Construction Paper
- (Optional) Glue. If you choose to not use glue, you will need card sleeves

#### Method 2 Process

1. Print the PDFs you've created.
2. At this point you have can glue the construction paper onto the back of the card page. It makes it easier to cut both pieces of paper accurately, but also involves gluing and waiting for it to dry. If you don't want to glue, you can hold the two pieces together with paper clips, or simply cut them separately.
3. Cut the cards along the lines.
4. (Optional) Round the corners.
5. (Optional) Sleeve the cards. If you didn't glue the construction paper onto the card page, you will need to sleeve the cards with the construction paper behind the card itself.



### Method 3 - Laminated Cards

This method creates sturdy cards and works with dual-sided cards, but also requires more tools. This is my preferred method, as it is fast and produces the same result every time.

It is based on the video [The only mtg proxy guide you need(none foil version) - by Crycry](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cjayDpUrgUk).

#### Method 3 Tools and Materials
- Same as [Method 1 Tools and Materials](#method-1-tools-and-materials)
- A Laminating Machine. It doesn't need to be expensive, just make sure it can handle the thickness of the laminating pouches you decide to use
- Laminating Pouches. The thickness depends on what you want to achieve and the thickness of the paper you use. I personally print on standard 80 gsm paper and use 125 micron pouches. That combination achieves virtually the same thickness and "bendiness" as official MtG cards
    - NOTE: Laminating pouches come as either gloss or matt. Gloss is cheaper and works just fine for these cards, as they will often be looked at by one person at a time. Matt is more for cards that will be looked at by multiple people from different angles such as MtG Proxies.

#### Method 3 Process

1. Print the PDFs you've created.
2. Laminate the pages. If you use the same type of paper and pouches as me (80 gsm / 125 micron), it is one card page to one laminating pouch.
3. Cut the cards along the lines.
4. (Optional) Round the corners.
5. (Optional) Sleeve the cards.
